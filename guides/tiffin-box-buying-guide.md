## The main types

| Type | Good at | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Stainless steel stacked carrier | Separating dal, sabzi, rice and rotis; durable for years; plastic-free | Most are not leak-proof without silicone seals; no insulation on their own |
| Insulated (vacuum) tiffin or food jar | Keeping hot food hot or cold food cold for hours | Must be preheated or prechilled to work well; heavier; fewer compartments |
| Bento-style compartment box | Children's tiffins with several small items | Shallow compartments leak with gravies; usually not insulated |
| Glass meal-prep containers | Reheating at work in a microwave; easy to see what is inside | Heavy and breakable in a school bag |
| Plastic containers | Light and cheap | Choose food-grade containers and only microwave those labelled microwave-safe |

## What to look for

- **A real seal.** Silicone gaskets on each tier if you pack dal or curry. Test with water before the first day.
- **The right size.** As a rough guide, young children manage about 400 to 600 ml of food in total; adults usually want 900 ml to 1.5 litres across tiers.
- **Heat retention.** If you pack hot food without a microwave at the other end, an insulated tier or food jar is not optional. See our [food safety guide]({{ '/guides/tiffin-food-safety.html' | relative_url }}).
- **Clasp and handle.** A firm clasp that a child can open but a bag cannot.
- **Easy to clean.** Fewer crevices, removable seals, dishwasher-safe if that matters to you.
- **Weight.** Steel and glass add up. For long commutes, mix one insulated tier with lighter containers.

## For children

Pick a box with compartments sized for finger food, a lid that opens with one hand, and space for an ice pack in the bag. Let them practise opening it at home. Label it: school lost-and-found boxes are full of nameless steel tiffins.

## For the office

If there is a microwave, glass or microwave-safe containers make reheating easy. If not, use a preheated insulated tiffin for hot food and a separate cold tier with ice packs for curd or salad.

## Caring for steel tiffins

Wash after every use in hot soapy water, remove and clean the seals regularly, and dry fully before stacking. A paste of baking soda and water lifts turmeric stains; avoid steel wool, which scratches the surface.
