## Is it worth it? The numbers first

Published guides for Indian home tiffin services put gross margins at roughly 40 to 50% per meal and net margins at 25 to 35% once delivery and overheads are paid. Starting from a home kitchen can cost as little as ₹10,000 to ₹50,000 for containers, vessels, insulated bags and the first groceries, and break-even typically arrives at about 25 to 30 regular customers.

In Canada, weekday lunch plans from established kitchens start at around CA$160 a month, which is roughly CA$8 a tiffin. That is the price you compete with.

The hard part is not cooking. In Toronto, the co-founder of one tiffin marketplace told reporters that sellers now outnumber customers. **Marketing and retention, not recipes, decide whether a tiffin business survives.** Plan for that from day one.

Run your own numbers in the [tiffin business calculator]({{ '/tools/tiffin-business-calculator.html' | relative_url }}) before you spend anything.

## Step 1: Pick a lane

The kitchens that last are specific. "Indian food" is not a lane; these are:

- Jain and no-onion-garlic tiffins for families who cannot eat out easily.
- High-protein vegetarian tiffins for gym-goers.
- Regional home cooking that restaurants ignore: Bengali, Konkani, Kerala, Sindhi, Telugu.
- Student plans near a campus, priced per week, with exam-week pauses.
- Office lunches in individual sealed boxes for a single business park.

Pick one lane, one area you can deliver to in under an hour, and one meal (lunch or dinner) to start.

## Step 2: Get legal

Rules differ by country and often by city. This table summarises the main requirement; always confirm with the authority before you trade.

| Where | What you need | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ontario, Canada | Notify your local public health unit before opening | Home kitchens must meet Ontario's Food Premises Regulation and can be inspected. Contact your municipality about home-business rules. |
| Québec, Canada | A MAPAQ food permit | On the island of Montréal, the City carries out food inspection. |
| British Columbia, Canada | Approval from your regional health authority | Surrey and the Fraser Valley fall under Fraser Health. |
| Alberta, Canada | A food handling permit from Alberta Health Services | Apply before selling prepared meals. |
| California, US | A MEHKO permit, where your county has opted in | Up to 30 meals a day, 90 a week and about US$100,000 a year. You need food manager certification, and ads must show your permit number and "Made in a Home Kitchen". |
| Most other US states | A licensed commercial or shared kitchen | Cottage food laws usually cover only low-risk foods such as baked goods, not cooked meals. |
| England, Wales, Northern Ireland | Register with your local council at least 28 days before trading | Registration is free. Expect an inspection and a public food hygiene rating. Scotland has an equivalent process. |
| India | FSSAI registration or licence via FoSCoS | From 1 April 2026, basic registration covers annual turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (it was ₹12 lakh). Your 14-digit number must appear on packaging. |
| Victoria, Australia | Register with your local council under the Food Act 1984 | The council classifies your business by risk and tells you what you need. |

On top of food registration, check three more things: business registration in your province, state or country; insurance that covers food sold from your home (many home policies exclude it); and sales tax. In Canada, for example, you must register for GST/HST once your taxable sales pass the small-supplier threshold of CA$30,000 over four consecutive quarters.

## Step 3: Price it properly

The price of a tiffin has to cover four things: food, packaging, delivery, and your time. A simple check:

1. Add up the food and packaging cost of one tiffin. Keep food under 40% of your price.
2. Add a monthly figure for delivery (fuel, a driver or a courier) and fixed costs (registration, gas and electricity, insurance, marketing).
3. Divide those monthly costs by what each customer contributes after food and packaging. That is your break-even customer count.

Worked example in Canadian dollars: a CA$10 tiffin with CA$3.50 of food and CA$0.60 of packaging leaves CA$5.90. Twenty-five subscribers taking 22 tiffins a month gives 550 tiffins and CA$3,245 of contribution. Take away CA$600 of delivery and CA$250 of fixed costs and you keep about CA$2,395 a month, before tax.

If an aggregator app takes 15 to 20% commission, that comes straight out of the CA$5.90. Price for it, or sell direct.

## Step 4: Build a menu rotation that scales

- Write a two-week rotation and publish it every Friday. Customers trust what they can see.
- Share bases across dishes: one onion-tomato masala, one tempering, one batch of dal, used three ways.
- Plan dishes that are good four hours later. Dry sabzis, firm dals and flatbreads travel; delicate gravies and fried snacks do not.
- Keep a Jain or no-onion-garlic pot separate if you offer it, and say so.

## Step 5: Packaging and delivery

Returnable steel tins feel premium and cost less over time, but you need a deposit and a pickup routine. Disposable containers are simpler for offices. Either way, label each tiffin with the customer's name and diet.

Start by delivering yourself within 3 to 5 km. A paid driver tends to make sense from around 30 customers. Group customers into routes and fix delivery windows; a route you run every day is cheaper than ten one-off trips.

## Step 6: Your first thirty customers

- **WhatsApp Business** for menus, orders and reminders. It is where your customers already are.
- **A Google Business Profile** so you appear for "tiffin service near me" searches.
- **One building at a time:** a PG or student residence, an office park, a hospital night shift.
- **A paid trial week** priced close to cost. Conversion from a good trial is your best marketing.
- **Referral credit** for every customer who brings a friend.
- **Listings** such as [TiffinBreak's kitchen listings]({{ '/list-your-kitchen.html' | relative_url }}), where customers arrive already knowing their city, diet and budget.

## Mistakes that sink new tiffin kitchens

- Pricing to beat the cheapest kitchen instead of pricing to cover costs and your time.
- Saying yes to every diet and every area in month one.
- No written menu, so every complaint becomes an argument.
- Ignoring food safety rules on hot and cold holding. See our [tiffin food safety guide]({{ '/guides/tiffin-food-safety.html' | relative_url }}).
- Running out of cash because customers pay late. Take payment in advance, weekly or monthly.
