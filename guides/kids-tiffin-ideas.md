## The four-part formula

A good school tiffin has four parts. Mix and match and you never need to think about it again.

| Part | Ideas |
|---|---|
| A main they can eat with their hands | Mini idli, paneer roll, paratha strips, sandwich quarters, chilla folded in four |
| Something with protein | Paneer, egg, chana, sprouts (steamed), dal-based snacks, curd in the cold tier |
| Fruit or crunchy vegetables | Cucumber sticks, carrot, grapes halved for little ones, apple slices with a squeeze of lemon |
| A small crunch or treat | Roasted makhana, a few nuts if the school allows them, homemade chikki |

## Make it easy to eat

- **Bite-sized beats impressive.** Five minutes into a twenty-minute break, nobody wants to wrestle with a whole paratha.
- **Separate wet and dry.** Soggy bread is the most common reason a tiffin comes home uneaten.
- **Lids small hands can open.** Practise at home with a new box before the first school day.
- **Familiar first, new second.** Put one new food beside a favourite, not instead of it.

## Picky eaters

- Let children choose between two options the night before. Choice buys cooperation.
- Serve the new food at home first, when nobody is watching the clock.
- Use dips: a little ketchup, chutney or curd makes unfamiliar vegetables less risky.
- Keep portions small. A finished small tiffin beats a half-eaten big one, and you learn what they actually like.
- Ask what came back and why. "It was soggy" and "I didn't have time" are both fixable.

## Nut-free schools

Many schools ban nuts. Swap peanuts in poha or lemon rice for roasted chana dal or pumpkin seeds, check that podi and chikki are nut-free, and read labels on packaged snacks.

## Keep it safe until the bell

Children's tiffins often sit in a classroom or locker for four or five hours. Pack anything with curd, paneer, egg or rice either hot in a preheated insulated tin or cold with two ice packs. Our [food safety guide]({{ '/guides/tiffin-food-safety.html' | relative_url }}) explains why, and the [safety checker]({{ '/tools/tiffin-safety-checker.html' | relative_url }}) gives a quick answer for any tiffin.
