Is my tiffin still safe?

Perishable food should not sit in the temperature danger zone for more than two hours, or one hour when it is hot out. Insulated tins and ice packs buy time. Enter your tiffin and see where it stands.

The rules behind the answer

  • Bacteria multiply fastest between 4°C and 60°C (40°F and 140°F), the danger zone.
  • Perishable food should spend no more than 2 hours in that zone, or 1 hour above 32°C (90°F).
  • For cold tiffins, pack at least two cold sources, such as two ice packs or one ice pack and a frozen water bottle.
  • For hot tiffins, fill an insulated tin with boiling water for a few minutes, empty it, then add food at 60°C (140°F) or hotter and keep the lid closed until lunch.
  • Throw away anything left over at the end of lunch rather than bringing it home to eat later.

The time allowances for insulated tins and ice packs in this checker are deliberately conservative rules of thumb built on that guidance. They are not a lab test of your container. When in doubt, throw it out.
